Paracetamol or ibuprofen for a fever: which, and when?

Both work. The better choice depends on your stomach, your kidneys, whether you’re pregnant — and what you’re actually trying to treat.

THE SHORT ANSWER
→Treat how you feel, not the number on the thermometer. A fever itself is usually the body doing its job.
→Paracetamol is the gentler first choice for most people, including in pregnancy.
→Ibuprofen also eases inflammation, but avoid it with stomach ulcers, kidney disease, or later pregnancy.
→Never go over the maximum daily dose on the label — and check cold remedies, which often contain paracetamol too.

Do you need to treat a fever at all?

A raised temperature is one of the ways your immune system fights infection. For most adults and children, bringing it down doesn’t help you recover faster — it simply makes you more comfortable. That’s a perfectly good reason to take something, but it means you don’t need to chase a “normal” reading.

Who should avoid which

Ask a pharmacist or doctor before taking ibuprofen if you have had a stomach ulcer, have kidney or heart disease, take blood thinners, or are more than 20 weeks pregnant. Some people with asthma find ibuprofen makes their breathing worse. Paracetamol needs extra care if you have liver disease or drink heavily.

● WHEN TO SEE A DOCTOR NOW
AD-FREE ZONE
• Any fever in a baby under 3 months
• A stiff neck, or pain looking at light
• A rash that doesn’t fade when pressed with a glass
• Confusion, drowsiness or a seizure
• Difficulty breathing
• Fever lasting more than 3 days
